ARI Announces Successful Prototype and Availability of Noncompetitive Follow-on Awards

April 1, 2025 · 6 min read · By ARI Communications

The Applied Research Institute (ARI) today announced the successful delivery of a prototype Acquisition Business Model under agreement with the Department of Defense Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office (CDAO)—and confirmed that agencies can now procure the resulting Rapid Acquisition Marketplace (RAM) model on a noncompetitive, follow-on basis.

A prototype that proved the model

The prototype was designed to answer a single question: can the Department buy emerging capability faster, more openly, and with broader participation—without sacrificing rigor? The results indicate that it can. RAM collected 880 non-traditional solutions, leading to $259.58 million in awards, demonstrating both the depth of the vendor pool and the model's ability to convert interest into contracts.

Adoption across the Department

Beyond the prototype's own results, the model was adopted by 47 DoD organizations, together accounting for $322.3 million in awards. That breadth of uptake is itself a finding: the approach is not a one-off, but a mechanism multiple organizations can apply to their own requirements.

Speed and rigor are not opposites. A well-designed marketplace delivers both—because the Warfighter Can't Wait.

Dramatically faster timelines

The headline operational result is time. Across procurements run through the model, the average Procurement Administrative Lead Time (PALT) was 51 days, with some procurements completed in as few as 8 to 14 days. Against traditional acquisition cycles measured in months or years, that is a step-change in how quickly mission owners can move from need to award.

  • 880 non-traditional solutions collected, leading to $259.58 million in awards.
  • 47 DoD organizations adopted the model, with $322.3 million in awards.
  • 51-day average PALT, with some procurements completed in 8–14 days.
  • 88% of solutions awarded through Tradewinds in FY2024 were innovative, non-traditional solutions.

Now available as a follow-on award

With the prototype successfully completed, agencies can procure the RAM model on a sole-source basis under 10 U.S.C. 4022(f). This noncompetitive follow-on authority means an organization that wants the proven capability can move directly to award, without re-running a full competition—removing a major source of delay while preserving the integrity of the original, competitively awarded prototype.

What it means for the mission

For program offices, the practical takeaway is straightforward: a vetted, high-performing acquisition model is now available off the shelf. For the broader innovation base—especially the non-traditional companies that make up the bulk of RAM's awards—it means a faster, more accessible path into the Department. ARI will continue to operate RAM as a neutral platform built to widen participation and compress the time from solution to fielded capability.
