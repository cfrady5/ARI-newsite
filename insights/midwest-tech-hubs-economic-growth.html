ARI's Midwest Tech Hubs Positioned to Support $8.5 Billion in Economic Growth by 2035

June 15, 2025 · 6 min read · By ARI Communications

An independent study projects that the Applied Research Institute's (ARI) Indiana innovation programs and tech hubs will support over $8.5 billion in economic growth over the next decade. The findings put a number to a strategy ARI has pursued for years: convert federal investment and regional strengths into durable, high-value economic activity across the Midwest.

From federal designation to regional return

The projection builds on a foundation already in place. ARI's programs have secured more than $150 million in federal funding across three tech hubs, each targeting a technology domain critical to national security and economic competitiveness. The study models how that initial investment, combined with private capital and institutional partnerships, compounds into broad regional growth by 2035.

Funding secured across three tech hubs

  • Silicon Crossroads — $73.4 million to strengthen the microelectronics industrial base.
  • Heartland BioWorks — $51 million to build the biomanufacturing workforce and ecosystem.
  • MACH H2 — $25 million to advance hydrogen and clean-energy infrastructure.
Federal investment is the spark; the return is measured in jobs, capability, and communities that can compete for a decade and beyond.

Quality jobs, not just headcount

The study projects that ARI's programs will support more than 19,000 jobs—and crucially, that these are high-value positions. Wages associated with these roles are expected to run at least 25 percent above the state average, reflecting the skilled, technology-intensive nature of microelectronics, biomanufacturing, and clean-energy work.

That wage premium matters. It means the growth is not simply additive; it raises the floor, attracts and retains talent, and seeds the kind of advanced industrial base that keeps a region competitive long after the initial grants are spent.

Extending the reach with Opportunity Indiana

The hubs do not operate in isolation. Through Opportunity Indiana, ARI provides pro bono services that help municipalities across the state compete for and win federal grants. By lowering the barrier for smaller communities to access competitive funding, the program broadens the geographic footprint of the growth the study describes—spreading opportunity beyond the largest metros.

A model for regional innovation

Taken together, the findings describe a repeatable model: a neutral operator aligns government, industry, and academia around shared outcomes, secures federal investment, and then runs the programs that turn that investment into jobs and capability. The $8.5 billion projection is the measure of what disciplined execution can deliver when the connective tissue is built to last.
